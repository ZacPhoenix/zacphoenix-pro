# Edge Waypoint - Website Copy, Version 2 (the human voice)

Same three-page structure and design system as [05-website-plan.md](05-website-plan.md). This version replaces the copy direction with actual draft copy, written the way you'd say it across a table. Names and places are placeholders - swap in yours and your town's.

## What changed from v1 and why

V1's copy was built from taglines: parallel phrasing, punchy fragments, the "Sometimes X. Sometimes Y." rhythm. That cadence reads as marketing at best and AI at worst, and your buyers have developed antibodies to both. V2 rules:

- **First person singular.** You're one person; "we" is a costume. "I" builds the exact trust the business runs on.
- **Specifics over slogans.** "The invoices someone retypes by hand every Thursday" beats "operational inefficiencies" every time. An owner recognizes their own Tuesday in a specific; they bounce off an abstraction.
- **Say the price of things, including your own limits.** Real people hedge and admit. "I don't know yet if I can help you" is more persuasive than any promise.
- **The read-aloud test (use it on every edit):** if you wouldn't say the sentence to a neighbor at a barbecue, rewrite it until you would. If it sounds like a billboard, cut it.

> Here's something most consultants won't tell you: about half of what I find doesn't need me at all.

**What working with me looks like (three short paragraphs, not steps-with-icons):**

> **First we talk.** Twenty minutes on the phone, free. You tell me what's bugging you. If I don't think I can help, I'll say so on that call and probably point you at something cheaper than me.
>
> **Then I come see it.** If it makes sense, I spend ninety minutes at your place with you and the people who actually do the work. We walk through where the hours go and put rough dollar figures on the worst of it. You keep the write-up whether you ever hire me or not.
>
> **Then we fix one thing.** Not ten things. One, in four weeks, for a fixed price we agree up front. We measure how the work went before, and how it goes after. If the new way isn't clearly better, I'll tell you to stop, in writing, and I won't charge you extra to find out.

## Page 2: How it works

**Opening:**

> Most consulting websites are vague about what you're actually buying. This page is the opposite. Here's exactly what working with me looks like, what it costs, and what you walk away with at each step. If you read this and think "that's not for us," you've saved us both a meeting.

**The visit (free, 90 minutes, at your place):**

> We sit down with you and two or three of your key people and walk through a normal week. Not "what are your pain points" - I'll ask things like "walk me through what happens when a job comes in on a Friday afternoon." People mention things they've stopped noticing. That's where the money usually is.
>
> Within two days you get a short write-up: the problems we found, roughly what each one costs you a year, and what I'd do about each. Some of those answers will be "buy this cheap tool" or "change this handoff" - free advice, yours to act on. The write-up is yours either way.

**The map ({$X}, two weeks):**

> If a few problems look worth a closer look, I spend about two weeks in the weeds. I shadow the people who do the work, look at the systems you're paying for (most businesses use about a fifth of what their software can do), and check whether your records are in good enough shape to build on - because if they're not, that's the real first job and I'll tell you so.
>
> You get a ranked list: what each problem costs, what fixing it would take, what I'd build or buy or change, and the order I'd do it in. Including, in plain terms, the ones I think you should leave alone.

**The fix ({$X}, four weeks, fixed):**

> We pick one problem - usually the one with the best ratio of money-saved to fuss. Before I build anything, we write down how the work goes today: how long it takes, how often it goes wrong. That's the bar the fix has to clear.
>
> Someone on your team owns the new way of working from week two - not me, because I go home and you don't. By week four, one of two things is true. Either the numbers beat the old way and you keep it, or they don't and I tell you to stop. You get that recommendation in writing either way. You're paying me for the honest answer, not for endless tinkering.

**Afterwards ({$X}/month, optional, cancel anytime):**

> Things that run on software drift. Systems update, volumes change, something that worked in March hiccups in August. For a monthly fee I keep an eye on what we built, fix the small stuff before you notice it, and send you a one-page note each month with the numbers. Most clients keep this; nobody's locked into it.

**A few questions I get a lot (answers in your speaking voice):**

> **Is this an AI thing?**
> Only when AI is honestly the right tool, which is less often than the ads suggest. Most of what I fix is duller than that - information getting retyped, things waiting on other things. When AI genuinely earns its keep (usually reading messy stuff like emails and PDFs), I use it, with a person checking its work.
>
> **Do I need to be technical?**
> No. If you can describe what annoys you, that's the whole job requirement.
>
> **Who owns what you build?**
> You do. It runs in your accounts, under your logins, documented in plain English. If I get hit by a bus, your stuff keeps working.
>
> **What if we're too small for this?**
> If the math doesn't work I'll tell you on the first call. It costs you twenty minutes to find out.

## Editing notes for keeping it human over time

- When you add case studies, write them as small stories with real numbers, not bullet lists. "Their office manager was spending Thursday mornings retyping work orders. Now she isn't. That's about $9,000 a year back" beats any metrics table.
- Keep contractions. Keep sentences that start with And or But. Vary sentence length - uniform punchiness is the tell.
- One orange CTA per screen, same words every time. The voice can be warm because the funnel is strict.
- Re-run the read-aloud test on anything you didn't write yourself - including anything I draft for you.
