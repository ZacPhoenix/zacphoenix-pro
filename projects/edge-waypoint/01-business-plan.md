# Edge Waypoint - Business Plan

## 1. Positioning

**Category:** Operations capability consultancy for local small-to-medium businesses. Not an "AI agency."

**The metaphor is the brand.** A waypoint is the next fixed point on a route - not the destination, not the whole map. Edge Waypoint finds a business's next waypoint: the single highest-value capability gap, and the smallest system that closes it. "Edge" carries double weight: the competitive edge clients gain, and the fact that you work at the edge - on site, in person, in their building, not from a dashboard three time zones away.

**Positioning statement:**

> Edge Waypoint helps local businesses find the few operational problems actually worth solving, then solves them with the smallest thing that works - a process fix, a feature you already pay for, a $40/month tool, an automation, or AI - in that order. Fixed prices, four-week builds, and a written recommendation to kill anything that doesn't beat its baseline.

**The differentiators, ranked by how hard they are to copy:**

1. **In person, local, repeatedly.** The market research is unambiguous: the giants (Microsoft's Copilot SMB campaign, OpenAI's consulting arm) generate top-of-funnel awareness but will never sit in a 12-person distributor's break room. Local presence is the moat AI cannot commoditize.
2. **Tool-agnostic honesty.** "AI is not always the answer" is a marketing weapon. Every SMB owner has now been pitched AI snake oil or lived through a dead pilot (78% of SMB AI pilots never reach production). The consultant who says "this one's a SaaS subscription, this one's a checklist, only this one needs a build" is the one who gets the referral.
3. **Decision discipline.** Pilots fail structurally, not technically: no owner, no baseline, no kill criteria. Edge Waypoint sells the decision process as part of the product - baselines documented before work starts, a named operator on the client side, and a binary scale-or-kill recommendation at week four, in writing, in the proposal.
4. **Minimum viable architecture.** Capabilities that drive the desired outcome, nothing more. Fewer moving parts = less maintenance = a client who still likes you in a year.

## 2. Target market

**Primary:** owner-led businesses, 5-50 employees, ops-heavy, within driving distance. Verticals where the economics work best (high admin burden, repeated document/communication workflows, money tied to response time):

- Professional services: accounting firms, law offices, insurance brokers, agencies
- Trades and contractors: HVAC, electrical, plumbing, landscaping, builders (quoting, scheduling, job documentation)
- Logistics and distribution, light manufacturing / job shops (order intake, status visibility)
- Clinics and practices: dental, veterinary, physio (intake, reminders, records hygiene)
- Property management and real estate (maintenance routing, tenant comms)

**Buyer:** the owner or GM. In SMB there is no "head of digital transformation" - the person who feels the pain signs the check. This is why the in-person workshop motion works: owners buy from people, and they buy when shown their own numbers.

**Disqualifiers (walk away politely):** businesses in active financial distress, owners shopping for a magic employee replacement, anyone who refuses to name an internal operator for a pilot, and pure custom-software-product asks (refer those out).

## 3. Service ladder

Each rung is productized, fixed-price, and exists to de-risk the next rung. Names create the productized feel.

| # | Product | Price (launch → established) | Duration | What the client gets |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Waypoint Workshop** | Free → $500 (credited to audit) | 90 min on site | Facilitated friction-mapping session + a 2-page **Waypoint Brief** within 48 hours: top 3 problems, rough annual cost of each, recommended next step |
| 1 | **Waypoint Map** (the audit) | $1,500-$3,500 → $5K-$8K | 2 weeks | Current-state workflow map, Friction Census with annualized costs, Opportunity Scorecard ranking, solution recommendation per problem (with explicit non-AI answers where they fit), 90-day roadmap, readout meeting |
| 2 | **Edge Pilot** (the build) | $4,000-$9,000 → $10K-$18K | 4 weeks, hard stop | One workflow shipped to production: documented baseline, named client operator trained, monitoring in place, and the **Pilot Decision One-Pager** - scale or kill, in writing |
| 3 | **Waypoint Watch** (the retainer) | $400-$1,200/mo → $1.5K-$3K/mo | Monthly | Monitoring and drift checks on everything shipped, monthly metrics report, fixes and small iterations, priority response, quarterly re-scan for the next waypoint |

**Pricing logic:**

- Launch prices sit deliberately below the market benchmarks from the research (SMB audits $2K-$8K, pilots $5K-$30K, retainers $1.5K-$30K/mo) because your first 5 clients are buying you references. Raise prices after every 2-3 completed pilots; the audit should reach market rate within 6 months.
- **Fixed fee everywhere.** The audit category is mature and the deliverables are standardized; fixed pricing aligns your incentive with finishing, and SMB owners hate open meters.
- Frame retainer pricing **per workflow watched, not per seat.** Owners mentally benchmark per-seat costs against $20/month consumer subscriptions and balk.
- The workshop is free at launch because it is your marketing. Charge for it once referrals outpace your calendar - a price on it then *increases* show-up quality.

**Rules that protect the ladder:**

- Never skip rungs for a new client, however eager. An owner who wants to "just build the thing" gets a compressed 1-week Map first. The Map is where you catch the "this is actually a SaaS subscription" cases that protect your reputation.
- Never bundle vaguely. Buyers are now explicitly coached to refuse retainers with vague deliverables and strategy decks without implementation. Every line item has a named artifact.
- One pilot at a time per client. Tool sprawl kills pilots; ship one before starting the next.

## 4. Go-to-market: the local funnel

The motion: **get in rooms → run workshops → convert to Maps → convert to Pilots → harvest referrals.**

**Channels, in priority order:**

1. **Referral partners (highest leverage).** Accountants, bookkeepers, MSPs/IT providers, fractional CFOs, and business bankers see inside dozens of local SMBs and are constantly asked "should we be using AI?" Give them a clean answer: a co-branded workshop they can gift to clients. Offer 10% of first-engagement revenue or simply reciprocal referrals. Five active partners ≈ a full pipeline.
2. **Owner watering holes.** Chamber of commerce, BNI/networking groups, trade association chapter meetings, Rotary. Join two, attend relentlessly, never pitch - offer the workshop.
3. **The local talk.** A 25-minute talk, "The Five Operations Problems Every [Vertical] Has - and Why Only One of Them Needs AI," delivered at association meetings and chamber lunches. The title does the positioning work. End with a workshop signup sheet.
4. **Direct outreach.** 10 personalized emails/LinkedIn messages per week to local owners (scripts in the engagement guide). Walk-ins work in trades - owners respect showing up.
5. **Case study flywheel.** After every pilot: a one-page, number-led local case study ("Cut quote turnaround from 5 days to 1 at a 14-person HVAC company"). Local names beat national logos with this audience.

**What you are NOT doing at launch:** content marketing at scale, paid ads, cold calling lists, a podcast. One person; rooms and referrals convert; the rest is procrastination with a dashboard.

## 5. Capacity and financial sketch (solo operator)

Working assumption: ~70% of your week is billable-adjacent at steady state; workshops and partner coffee meetings are sales time.

| Phase | Monthly mix | Monthly revenue (launch pricing) |
|---|---|---|
| Months 1-3 (prove it) | 4-6 workshops, 1-2 Maps, 0-1 Pilot | $2K-$8K |
| Months 4-6 (ladder works) | 4 workshops, 2 Maps, 1 Pilot, 2-3 Watch clients | $10K-$18K |
| Months 7-12 (raise prices) | 2-3 Maps, 1-2 Pilots, 6-10 Watch clients | $18K-$35K |

**The retainer is the business.** Every shipped pilot should convert to Waypoint Watch (target 80%+ attach rate - position it in the pilot proposal as the default, since "no monitoring" is a named killer of AI deployments and only ~21% of organizations have any mature governance). At 10 Watch clients × $1K average, $10K/month recurs before you sell anything that month. Watch revenue is also your buffer against pilot scheduling gaps.

**Costs are trivial by design:** tooling stack <$300/month (see accelerators doc), E&O + general liability insurance (~$1-2K/yr - non-negotiable, see risk section), chamber/association dues, gas and coffee.

## 6. Risk register

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Liability for AI output (a German court just held Google liable for its AI's answers; the chain runs from your client's customer to you) | E&O insurance before first pilot; contract clause limiting liability to fees paid; human-review-before-send as the default architecture for anything customer-facing; never deploy unreviewed customer-facing AI for regulated businesses |
| Pilot fails technically | The Solution Ladder bias toward rungs 1-4 means most builds are integrations, not AI bets; baseline + 4-week box caps the client's downside; a documented kill is a kept promise, not a failure |
| Client data handling | Self-hostable tools where it matters (n8n), a written data-handling one-pager clients sign, never train on or retain client data, keys live in the client's accounts not yours |
| Feast/famine solo pipeline | Workshops are cheap to run and keep the top of funnel warm even mid-delivery; Watch retainer smooths revenue |
| Scope creep | Fixed price + named artifacts + the change rule in the engagement guide ("new scope = new waypoint, written up separately") |
| You become the single point of failure | Everything you build is documented in the client's own workspace; the operator is trained; Watch clients get a runbook - this is also why clients renew rather than feel hostage |

## 7. 90-day launch plan

**Days 1-15 - Build the machine:**
- Stand up the ops workspace (starter kit in this repo): CLAUDE.md, skills, templates, CRM, calendar booking
- Finalize workshop kit; rehearse the 90-minute facilitation twice on friendly businesses (free, in exchange for feedback and a testimonial)
- Insurance, simple service agreement reviewed by a lawyer, business banking
- One-page website: what you do, the ladder, workshop booking link. Nothing more.

**Days 16-45 - Fill rooms:**
- Join two networking groups; book coffee with 10 potential referral partners (accountants, MSPs, bookkeepers)
- Run 6-8 workshops (including the two rehearsals). Target: 3 paid Maps booked
- Deliver Maps within their 2-week windows; every Map readout proposes one Edge Pilot

**Days 46-90 - Prove the ladder:**
- Deliver 1-2 Edge Pilots end to end, including the decision one-pager
- Convert pilots to Waypoint Watch
- Write the first two case studies; book the first local talk using them
- Review pricing: if close rate on Maps exceeds ~60%, raise prices 25%

**Success criteria at day 90:** 2 shipped pilots with documented baselines beaten (or honestly killed), 2+ Watch retainers active, 2 referral partners who have each sent at least one lead, and a calendar with workshops on it.
