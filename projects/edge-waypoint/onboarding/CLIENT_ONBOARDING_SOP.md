# Edge Waypoint — Client Onboarding SOP

Standard operating procedure for onboarding a new Edge Waypoint client, from the moment they sign through the end of the first week.

## Why this exists

Most clients we lose, we lose in the first week, before we have built anything.

The deal closes, the deposit hits, everyone is pumped. Then nothing happens for a few days while we "get organized." That silence is where buyer's remorse creeps in. The client starts wondering if they made the right call, and we have not even started building.

Onboarding is not paperwork to get through. It is the part of the relationship that protects the entire project. The build is what they paid for, and the first week is what makes them trust us enough to let us build it.

This SOP is the system we run from the moment they sign through the first week.

## How to use this

- Treat every box below as a hard gate. Do not skip ahead.
- Each phase has an owner, a target timing, and a definition of done.
- Copy the checklist into the client's record in the tracker and check items off there.
- If an item does not apply to a specific client, mark it N/A in writing. Do not leave it blank.

## Roles

- **Owner:** runs onboarding end to end and is accountable for the checklist.
- **Builder:** does the technical setup, mapping, and the first milestone.
- **Point of contact (client side):** the one person on the client side who can grant access and make decisions.

---

## Phase 1 — Day 0: the moment they sign

**Target:** all done the same day they sign.

- [ ] Send a welcome message the same day. Make it personal, not a copy-paste template.
- [ ] Send the invoice or payment link.
- [ ] Confirm the deposit landed.
- [ ] Make sure the contract is signed and filed.
- [ ] Spin up the comms channel (Slack channel or WhatsApp group) and invite them.
- [ ] Send the kickoff booking link. Aim for a call within 2 to 3 days.
- [ ] Send a short "here is what happens next" message so they are never guessing.

**Done when:** deposit is confirmed, contract is filed, comms channel is live, and the kickoff is booked.

## Phase 2 — Before the kickoff call

**Target:** complete before the call starts.

- [ ] Send a prep message listing exactly what logins and access to have ready.
- [ ] Re-read the sales call notes so you walk in sharp.
- [ ] Build a simple agenda for the call.
- [ ] Create their project folder in Drive.
- [ ] Create their record in Airtable or the tracker.

**Done when:** the client knows what to bring, and the folder, record, and agenda all exist.

## Phase 3 — On the kickoff call

**Target:** during the call.

- [ ] Record it (Fireflies or Loom) so you have a single source of truth.
- [ ] Get access to every tool and account live on the call.
- [ ] Walk through the exact process you are automating, step by step.
- [ ] Confirm the outcome and the number that proves it worked.
- [ ] Lock the build timeline and the milestones.
- [ ] Set the communication rules: where, how often, and expected response time.
- [ ] Confirm who your point of contact is during the build.
- [ ] Cover the edge cases and what happens when something breaks.

**Done when:** the call is recorded, access is granted, and scope, timeline, and the success number are all agreed out loud.

## Phase 4 — Right after the kickoff

**Target:** same day as the call.

- [ ] Send a recap message in writing: scope, outcome, and timeline.
- [ ] Map the full workflow from the recording before you build anything.
- [ ] Send the workflow map back so they confirm you understood it.
- [ ] Chase down any access or logins you are still missing.
- [ ] Set up any accounts or subscriptions the build needs.
- [ ] Put the milestones in the tracker with real dates.

**Done when:** the recap is sent, the workflow map is confirmed by the client, and milestones have real dates in the tracker.

## Phase 5 — First 7 days

**Target:** within the first week.

- [ ] Start on the first milestone.
- [ ] Send a first progress Loom by day 2 or 3 to show momentum.
- [ ] Send one quick mid-week check-in message.
- [ ] Get one visible piece in front of them inside the first week.
- [ ] Flag any blocker or timeline risk early. Do not sit on it.

**Done when:** the first milestone is underway, the client has seen visible progress, and any risks have been raised early.

---

## Definition of a successful onboarding

By the end of the first week the client should:

1. Have heard from us the same day they signed, and consistently since.
2. Have a confirmed, written understanding of scope, outcome, and timeline.
3. Have seen at least one visible piece of real progress.
4. Trust us enough to let us build the rest without second-guessing the decision.
