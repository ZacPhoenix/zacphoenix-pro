# Cast & Engagement Profiles ("Answer Key")

This file documents the intended, consistent characteristics of every speaker in
the transcripts. It is the labeled ground truth the engagement/coaching
prototype can be evaluated against. Every transcript honors these profiles, so
patterns are trackable *across* lessons and *over* the day.

All characters are fictional. Labels (Student A, Teacher 1) are used instead of
names on purpose.

---

## Students

Engagement here is deliberately multidimensional. Two students can both "look
engaged" while producing very different mic signals — a key reason quiet or
struggling students get missed by naïve talk-time metrics.

### Student A — the high achiever
- **Baseline engagement:** High and consistent.
- **Signal:** Frequent, fast hand-raising; long, accurate answers; strongest in
  Math and Science.
- **Watch-for:** Can dominate discussion and answer before peers get think time.
- **Coaching hook:** A teacher over-relying on A's hand is a classic equity gap.

### Student B — steady and dependable
- **Baseline engagement:** Moderate-high, very stable across subjects.
- **Signal:** Participates when invited, rarely blurts, often helps peers in
  group work. Reliable "barometer" student.
- **Watch-for:** Low variance — useful as a control when reading other students.

### Student C — quiet and attentive
- **Baseline engagement:** High *internally*, near-zero *verbally*.
- **Signal:** Almost never volunteers aloud; strong written work; when cold-
  called (gently) gives thoughtful answers. Blossoms in ELA writing.
- **Watch-for:** Talk-time metrics badly *underestimate* C. This is the headline
  "engagement is not the same as participation" case.
- **Coaching hook:** Individualization — private check-ins, written response
  options, low-stakes invitations.

### Student D — distractible
- **Baseline engagement:** Low-moderate, spiky.
- **Signal:** Off-task side talk, doodling, needs repeated redirection; engages
  sharply during hands-on / kinesthetic tasks (Science lab, manipulatives).
- **Watch-for:** Engagement is *format-dependent*, not global. Rises in Science.
- **Coaching hook:** Front-load hands-on entry points; proximity; task chunking.

### Student E — impulsive and enthusiastic
- **Baseline engagement:** High energy, high but *dysregulated*.
- **Signal:** Blurts answers, interrupts, calls out — genuinely excited,
  especially in Science. Not defiant; impulse control.
- **Watch-for:** High talk-time can read as "great engagement" but crowds others.
- **Coaching hook:** Structured turn-taking, wait-time protocols, channeling
  energy into a role.

### Student F — the math-anxious student
- **Baseline engagement:** Subject-dependent. Shuts down in Math; strong and
  articulate in Social Studies discussion.
- **Signal:** Negative self-talk ("I'm just bad at this"), goes silent when
  frustrated, avoids eye contact / volunteering in Math.
- **Watch-for:** A steep *within-day* and *within-subject* engagement drop is
  the flag, not a low daily average.
- **Coaching hook:** Individualization — growth-mindset language, scaffolding,
  separating "I can't yet" from "I can't."

### Student G — the multilingual learner
- **Baseline engagement:** High effort, growing confidence; hesitant to speak.
- **Signal:** Participates more with visuals, sentence frames, wait time, and
  partner talk; language load in fast lectures suppresses output.
- **Watch-for:** Engagement tracks *scaffolding availability*, not ability.
- **Coaching hook:** Pacing, sentence stems, pre-teaching vocabulary, checking
  for understanding beyond "does everyone get it?"

### Student H — socially motivated
- **Baseline engagement:** High in collaborative formats, low in lecture.
- **Signal:** Thrives and leads in group/partner work; drifts into social side
  talk during direct instruction.
- **Watch-for:** Engagement is *format-dependent* like D, but driven by social
  connection rather than kinesthetics.
- **Coaching hook:** Cooperative structures with individual accountability.

## Teachers

Each teacher is competent and well-intentioned, and each has a realistic,
*specific* growth edge — the coaching signal the prototype should be able to
surface. Coaching edges are shown through behavior in the transcripts, not
narrated.

### Teacher 1 — Homeroom & Math
- **Strengths:** Warm, builds routine, strong content knowledge.
- **Growth edge:** *Wait time & equity of voice.* Tends to call on the fastest
  hands (A, E), under-reaches C, F, G. Moves on before F recovers.

### Teacher 2 — English Language Arts
- **Strengths:** Inclusive structures (think-pair-share), values every voice.
- **Growth edge:** *Pace.* Discussion runs fast; language-heavy transitions
  leave G behind; occasionally accepts the first right answer and moves on.

### Teacher 3 — Science
- **Strengths:** High-energy, hands-on, contagious enthusiasm; D and E light up.
- **Growth edge:** *Managing airtime.* Lets E blurt and dominate; safety/
  procedure directions compete with excitement; quieter students recede.

### Teacher 4 — Social Studies
- **Strengths:** Skilled discussion facilitator; draws out F beautifully;
  connects content to students' lives.
- **Growth edge:** *Pacing & closure.* Runs long, rushes the last few minutes,
  skips the exit check — so understanding for C and G goes unverified.

## Intended cross-lesson patterns (what a good model should find)

- **C** looks disengaged by talk-time but is highly engaged; verify via written
  work and quality-when-invited.
- **D** and **H** are format-dependent; both peak away from lecture (D in
  hands-on Science, H in collaborative work).
- **F**'s engagement is subject-specific: a sharp Math dip, a Social Studies
  peak. A daily average hides both.
- **G**'s output rises and falls with scaffolding, not with the material's
  difficulty.
- **Time-of-day** matters: energy is highest at Homeroom/Math, dips in the
  post-lunch Social Studies block.
- **Teacher 1** demonstrates an equity-of-voice gap the same day **Teacher 2**
  demonstrates an inclusive structure — a natural contrast for coaching output.
