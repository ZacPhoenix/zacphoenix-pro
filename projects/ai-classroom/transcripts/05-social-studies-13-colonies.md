## Introduction

**Content.** Students compare the three colonial regions — **New England, Middle,
and Southern** — and reason about how **geography and climate shaped everyday
life and work** (farming, trade, crafts). The block uses a primary-source image
and a structured "Would you rather live in…?" discussion to build historical
empathy and evidence-based reasoning.

**Agenda.**
1. Do-now: map warm-up, label the three regions (5 min)
2. Primary-source image analysis: a colonial town scene (10 min)
3. Guided discussion: how geography shaped each region's work (12 min)
4. Structured "Would you rather?" discussion by region (13 min)
5. *(Intended)* exit ticket: one region, one reason (5 min)

**Structure.** This is the **post-lunch block** — expect a class-wide energy dip
and a slower start. It is a discussion-heavy lesson, the format where **Student
F** is strongest (contrast sharply with F's Math block). It also shows **Teacher
4's** facilitation strength and the **pacing/closure** growth edge: the lesson
runs long and the exit check gets squeezed.

**Learning outcomes.** Students will (1) name a defining feature of each colonial
region and (2) explain one way geography influenced daily life there.
**Success criterion (intended exit ticket):** name one region and give one
geography-based reason for its main type of work.

> Research note: F should be markedly more engaged here than in Math — this is
> the "engagement is subject-specific, not a fixed trait" case. Also flag the
> closure: the exit check is cut off, so understanding for C and G is left
