## Introduction

**Content.** A hands-on investigation of **states of matter** and phase change.
Groups observe ice melting and water vapor condensing on a cold surface, record
observations, and reason about **energy transfer** (adding/removing heat) and the
idea that **matter is conserved** through a phase change.

**Agenda.**
1. Phenomenon hook: a "sweating" cold cup — where does the water come from? (5 min)
2. Safety briefing (hot water station, goggles) (4 min)
3. Lab stations: melting ice + condensation on a chilled can (18 min)
4. Record observations on the group sheet (6 min)
5. Whole-group sense-making: build the explanation together (10 min)
6. Clean-up and exit question (2 min)

**Structure.** This is the **hands-on / kinesthetic block**, where format-
dependent engagement flips: expect **D** to re-engage sharply and **E** to run
hot. It is also where **Teacher 3's** enthusiasm shines and where the airtime-
management growth edge appears. Groups: {A, F}, {B, C}, {D, E}, {G, H}.

**Learning outcomes.** Students will (1) describe melting and condensation as
energy-driven state changes, (2) record accurate observations, and (3) explain
that the water on the cold cup came from the air, not through the cup.
**Success criterion:** the exit question correctly identifies condensation as
water from the air.
