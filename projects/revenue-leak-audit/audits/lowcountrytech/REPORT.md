# Website Revenue-Leak Audit
## Lowcountry Technologies — lowcountrytech.com

**Prepared:** August 6, 2026
**Pages reviewed:** 13 pages including the homepage, all five service pages, the services overview, About, Contact, and three location pages

---

### The Short Version

This is one of the stronger local business sites we review: the homepage says exactly what you do and where, real Google reviews from named customers appear on almost every page, and your phone number is tappable on every page. The leaks are in the last step — the moment a visitor decides to act. The single most valuable fix: give the "Schedule a Consultation" button a real calendar to land on, so the prospect who visits at 9 PM books a slot instead of drifting to the next provider who answers first.

**Leaks found:** 7 &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Quick wins:** 6 &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Biggest leak:** consultations that can't actually be scheduled

**Estimated annual revenue at risk: $8,900 – $210,000** (conservative floor to upside; every number is an estimate built from stated assumptions and published market benchmarks — see "What the leaks cost" below). Total cost to fix all seven: **$1,150 – $3,740**, which the floor estimate alone repays in under five months.

---

### Where Revenue Is Leaking

#### 1. "Schedule a Consultation" can't actually schedule anything
**Impact: High · Fix difficulty: Quick win · Est. $2,880 – $60,000/yr**

**What's happening:** The homepage's main button says "Schedule a Consultation" and the header offers a "Free IT Assessment" — but both land on the same general contact form at [lowcountrytech.com/contact](https://lowcountrytech.com/contact/). Nothing lets a visitor pick a time, and the form mentions neither the consultation nor the assessment.

**Why it costs you customers:** Industry call data shows about 43% of inquiries to small businesses happen outside business hours, and 78% of business buyers end up choosing whichever provider responds first. The office manager comparing IT providers at 9 PM wants to book Tuesday at 10 AM — if she can't, the tab closes and the first competitor to answer tomorrow wins.

**The fix:** Add a free scheduling link (Microsoft Bookings or Calendly) behind both buttons, offering a 15-minute intro call. Keep the form as the alternative.

#### 2. On phones, your homepage's only button sits two screens down
**Impact: High · Fix difficulty: Quick win · Est. $1,440 – $33,750/yr**

**What's happening:** On a phone, the header and the large headline fill the first two screens; the "Schedule a Consultation" button only appears after two full swipes. The first screen offers no action except the phone number. (Desktop is fine — the button is immediately visible.)

**Why it costs you customers:** Around 57% of local searches happen on a phone, so most first-time visitors see the phone-screen version. A prospect skimming three IT providers from a job site or a lobby gives each site seconds. The one where the next step is visible without scrolling gets the tap.

**The fix:** Tighten the mobile headline to two lines and pull the button onto the first screen, next to the phone number.

#### 3. Cybersecurity has no page of its own
**Impact: High · Fix difficulty: Project · Est. $1,440 – $30,000/yr**

**What's happening:** Cybersecurity is named second in your homepage's own description of the business — "managed IT support, cybersecurity, business phone systems…" — and your blog even targets the search "How to Choose a Cybersecurity Company in Savannah." But there is no cybersecurity service page. Managed IT, VoIP, alarm, and cabling each have one; cybersecurity exists only as mentions inside other pages and blog posts.

**Why it costs you customers:** The Savannah business owner who just received a cyber-insurance questionnaire — or a scare — searches for cybersecurity help specifically. Right now that search lands on a blog post at best, a competitor's dedicated page at worst. These are the buyers who sign full managed contracts.

**The fix:** Build one cybersecurity services page (assessments, monitoring, MFA, training, insurance-compliance help), linked from the Services menu, with the same FAQ treatment your location pages already do well.

#### 4. Google promises pricing your site never shows
**Impact: Medium · Fix difficulty: Quick win · Est. $1,440 – $30,000/yr**

**What's happening:** Your managed IT page's search listing reads: "Flat monthly per-user pricing, 24/7 monitoring, and a live local help desk. See exactly what is included in Lowcountry Technologies managed IT plans." The page explains the pricing *model*, and the FAQ answers "How is managed IT priced?" — but no number appears anywhere. (The alarm page, by contrast, states $29.99/month.)

**Why it costs you customers:** A buyer clicking that listing expects a number. Arriving to none feels like a bait-and-switch and sends price-shoppers back to the results. Publishing an honest range ("most clients invest $X–$Y per user, per month") pre-qualifies leads and builds on the straight-talk tone your contact page already sets.

**The fix:** Add a typical per-user monthly range and what's included to the managed IT page — or soften the search snippet to match the page.

#### 5. The visitor with an emergency finds no fast lane
**Impact: Medium · Fix difficulty: Quick win · Est. $1,440 – $22,500/yr**

**What's happening:** The homepage never says "24/7," "urgent," or "emergency." Round-the-clock coverage is mentioned only inside the managed IT page ("…providing 24/7 support…").

**Why it costs you customers:** Businesses switch IT providers at crisis moments — server down, ransomware note, phones dead. That visitor scans your homepage for a signal you handle urgent situations. Silence reads as "9 to 5," and the crisis buyer — the easiest full-contract win there is — keeps looking.

**The fix:** Add one line near the top of the homepage: "IT emergency? Call (912) 335-0175 — most calls answered live." You already make the "answered live" promise on the contact page; move it to where the urgent visitor looks first.

#### 6. Your Services overview page is a dead end
**Impact: Medium · Fix difficulty: Quick win · Est. $120 – $30,000/yr**

**What's happening:** The Services page ([lowcountrytech.com/services](https://lowcountrytech.com/services/)) — a main menu item — describes six service areas but contains no button, no "get in touch," no next step of any kind. It's also the only key page with no customer reviews on it.

**Why it costs you customers:** A visitor on this page is comparison-shopping across everything you do — exactly the "one local partner" buyer your homepage courts. When the page ends, they're left to find their own way to act, and some simply don't.

**The fix:** Add a closing call-to-action block (phone + consultation button) and the same review strip your other pages carry.

#### 7. Your phone-systems page asks buyers to "Get 3CX Now"
**Impact: Medium · Fix difficulty: Quick win · Est. $120 – $3,600/yr**

**What's happening:** On [lowcountrytech.com/voip-and-unified-communications](https://lowcountrytech.com/voip-and-unified-communications/), the one conversion button reads "Get 3CX Now."

**Why it costs you customers:** The buyer searched "business phone systems Savannah," not "3CX" — the vendor name means nothing to them, and an unfamiliar word on the only button creates hesitation at the exact moment of action. You'd never caption your alarm button "Get DSC Now."

**The fix:** Rename it to what the customer is buying: "Get a Phone System Quote" or "Upgrade My Business Phones."

The method: how many qualified inquiries a year each leak plausibly loses (conservative bands), times a 10–25% consultation-to-client rate, times the annual value of the client type that leak loses ($14,400–$30,000/yr for a managed IT client at market per-user rates; less for phone/alarm work).

### Also Worth Noting

- The About page ends with a blog-style "leave a comment" box — a leftover setting worth switching off.
- Two different buttons ("Free IT Assessment" and "Schedule a Consultation") promise two different things but lead to the same form; pick one offer and name it consistently.
- No guarantee or response-time promise appears anywhere on the site — your "Most calls answered live" line is one; consider making it a headline commitment.
- No option to text you — for the majority-mobile audience, a "text us" link is a low-effort channel some buyers strongly prefer.

---

### What to Fix First

1. Put a real scheduler behind "Schedule a Consultation" — captures the after-hours prospect who books instead of bouncing.
2. Pull the mobile homepage button onto the first screen — the majority of your visitors act without scrolling.
3. Rename "Get 3CX Now" and add a closing call-to-action to the Services page — two ten-minute fixes that unblock ready buyers.
4. Add an urgent-help line to the homepage — wins the crisis switcher, the highest-value lead you get.
5. Build the cybersecurity page — the one project on the list, and the one that captures whole-contract buyers you currently hand to competitors.
