# Website Revenue-Leak Audit
## Lowcountry Technologies — lowcountrytech.com

**Prepared:** August 6, 2026
**Pages reviewed:** 12 pages including the homepage, Services, Managed IT, VoIP, Network & Cabling, Alarm, Warehouse & Logistics, About, Contact, and three location pages

---

### The Short Version

Your site is in better shape than most in your industry: your phone number is tap-to-call in the header of every single page, your BBB A+ and 5.0-star Google reviews are displayed with real customer names, your blog is current, and the contact page copy is genuinely excellent. The leaks aren't in the foundation — they're in the places where a ready-to-buy visitor gets handed off, stalled, or sent somewhere else.

The three that cost you the most: your main menu points to the weaker of your two Managed IT pages, your VoIP page's only button sends visitors to a vendor's website instead of to you, and your site promises "24/7 support" while the only hours it publishes are Monday–Friday 8–5. All three are fixable in an afternoon.

**Leaks found:** 9 &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Quick wins:** 7 &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Biggest leak:** Your best Managed IT page isn't in the menu

---

### Where Revenue Is Leaking

#### 1. Your main menu sends visitors to the weaker of your two Managed IT pages
**Impact: High · Fix difficulty: Quick win**

**What's happening:** You have two Managed IT pages. The one in your menu, `/managed-it-services/`, opens with "Empowering Your Business with Proactive, Customized IT Management" and has a single "Contact Us" button at the bottom. The one *not* in your menu, `/managed-it-services-savannah-ga/`, opens with "When your server goes down at 9 a.m. on a Tuesday, you don't want to wait on hold with a national help desk three time zones away" — and has a "Call (912) 335-0175" button right in the hero, plus your Google reviews.

**Why it costs you customers:** Managed IT is your flagship service, so this menu item gets more clicks than any other. Everyone who uses your menu lands on the vaguer page with the weaker call to action, while your genuinely persuasive version sits where almost nobody finds it.

**The fix:** Point the "Managed IT Services" menu item at the Savannah page, and fold anything worth keeping from the old page into it. One page, your best writing, in the menu slot it deserves.

---

#### 2. Your VoIP page's only button sends visitors to 3CX instead of to you
**Impact: High · Fix difficulty: Quick win**

**What's happening:** The only action button on your VoIP & Unified Communications page reads "Get 3CX Now" and links to `3cx.com/phone-system/download-phone-system/`. There is no call button and no quote request anywhere in the body of that page.

**Why it costs you customers:** Someone reading your phone-systems page is one of the most valuable visitors on your site — they're actively shopping for business phones. That button takes them off your website and drops them into a software download page, where they either try to set it up themselves or shop around. Even with your reseller ID attached, you've traded a conversation you could have won for a click you can't follow up on.

**The fix:** Make the primary button "Call (912) 335-0175" or "Get a Phone System Quote," matching what already works on your Pooler and Warehouse pages. Keep the 3CX link if it's useful, but make it a secondary text link — not the main event.

---

#### 3. You promise "24/7 support" but the only hours you publish are Monday–Friday, 8–5
**Impact: High · Fix difficulty: Quick win**

**What's happening:** Your Managed IT page says you provide "24/7 support." Your Contact page says "Hours: Monday–Friday, 8:00 AM–5:00 PM." Your Pooler page carries both claims on the same page. The word "emergency" doesn't appear on your Contact page at all.

**Why it costs you customers:** "What happens when our server goes down at 9 on a Saturday night?" is the question every business owner asks before switching IT providers. Right now your site gives two different answers, so a careful prospect either assumes the worst or calls a competitor who states it plainly. The contradiction also undercuts the trust your BBB rating and reviews are working hard to build.

**The fix:** Decide what your real after-hours coverage is and say it in one sentence, in the same words, everywhere: on Contact, on Managed IT, and on the location pages. If after-hours support is a plan feature, that's a selling point — name it.

---

#### 4. Seven services you describe have no page to send anyone to
**Impact: High · Fix difficulty: Project**

**What's happening:** Your Services page describes nine services, but only two have pages of their own. There's no page for IT Support & Helpdesk, Security Assessments, Endpoint Protection, Business Continuity, Access Control Systems, IT Strategy & Planning, or Security Cameras — and none of those nine descriptions link anywhere. Cybersecurity is the clearest example: you mention it nine times on your homepage and name it in your main headline, but there's no cybersecurity page anywhere on the site.

**Why it costs you customers:** Someone searching "access control installation Savannah" or "cybersecurity for small business Savannah" has nothing on your site to find or land on. These are high-value jobs you're equipped to do, and the searches go to competitors who built the page.

**The fix:** Start with the two highest-value gaps — cybersecurity and access control/cameras. Give each a real page built like your Pooler page: specific problem, what's included, local proof, and a call button. Then work down the list.

#### 5. Your service pages have weaker calls to action than your location pages
**Impact: Medium · Fix difficulty: Quick win**

**What's happening:** Your Pooler and Warehouse & Logistics pages open with a "Call (912) 335-0175" button plus a second action ("Free Consultation," "Request a Site Survey"), and repeat the call button further down. Your Managed IT, Network & Cabling, and Alarm pages each have a single generic "Contact Us" at the bottom. Your main Services page has no call or contact button in the body at all.

**Why it costs you customers:** Whoever built your location pages got this right — those pages ask for the call, twice, in plain language. Your core service pages are still asking visitors to go find the contact page themselves. Every extra step between "I'm convinced" and "I'm calling" loses people.

**The fix:** Copy the pattern you already own. Put a "Call (912) 335-0175" button in the hero of each service page and a specific second action at the bottom — "Get a Cabling Quote," "Request an Alarm Assessment."

---

#### 6. Your Google reviews are missing from your Services and About pages
**Impact: Medium · Fix difficulty: Quick win**

**What's happening:** Your 5.0-star Google review widget — with real, named reviews like "These guys are miracle workers. Nick is a Genius." — appears on your homepage, Managed IT, VoIP, Alarm, Cabling, and Contact pages. It's missing from `/services/` and `/about/`.

**Why it costs you customers:** About is the page people open specifically to decide whether to trust you, and Services is where they're comparing what you do against another provider's list. Those are the two moments your reviews do the most work, and they're the two pages where they're absent.

**The fix:** Add the same review widget to both pages. On About, place it right after the "locally owned and operated" section so the proof lands immediately after the claim.

---

#### 7. Your contact form never tells anyone how fast you'll reply
**Impact: Medium · Fix difficulty: Quick win**

**What's happening:** Your Contact page explains the process well — "a short conversation about your environment, a free assessment if it's a fit, and a fixed-price proposal in plain English." But it never says how quickly someone hears back. The form also requires a phone number.

**Why it costs you customers:** Silence after hitting Submit is where leads go cold. A prospect with a problem today will fill out three forms and go with whoever answers first — and a stated timeframe is often what stops them from filling out the other two. Requiring a phone number also turns away the researcher who isn't ready to be called yet.

**The fix:** Add one line under the form: "We reply within one business hour, Monday–Friday." Make the phone field optional, or label it "Phone (fastest way to reach you)."

---

#### 8. Your strongest sales pitch is buried on the About page
**Impact: Medium · Fix difficulty: Quick win**

**What's happening:** Three paragraphs down your About page sits the best writing on your site: "Not a franchise, not a branch office, and not a white-labeled front for somebody else's help desk... a team that picks up the phone, already knows your network, and can be at your door quickly because we work and live here too." Above it are two paragraphs of general copy about empowering businesses in the digital age. Your homepage's three feature blocks are similarly general, with buttons labeled "Explore Now," "Experience the difference," and "Discover More Solutions."

**Why it costs you customers:** That buried paragraph is your entire competitive argument against the national providers you're competing with, and it's written in language a business owner recognizes instantly. Most visitors never reach the About page, and the ones who do have to read past filler to find it.

**The fix:** Move that "not a franchise, not a branch office" language up to the homepage, just under your headline, and put it at the top of the About page too. Replace the vague button labels with what the visitor actually gets.

---

#### 9. Every photo on your site is stock or generated — there's no picture of your actual team
**Impact: Medium · Fix difficulty: Project**

**What's happening:** The imagery across your site comes from stock libraries and AI generation (the About page photo is a stock file, and several others are generated assets). There are no photos of your technicians, your Rincon office, your vehicles, or work you've completed.

**Why it costs you customers:** Your whole argument is that you're local, real, and will show up in person — and the pictures on the page are the same ones any national competitor could buy. Prospects can't see the people they'd be trusting with their network. Real photos are one of the fastest ways to make "we're your neighbors" believable.

**The fix:** Get one session of real photos — the team, the office, a technician on a job, a finished cabling or camera install. Put faces on the About page and a real job photo on each service page.

---

### Also Worth Noting

- Your footer's "Our Services" list omits Warehouse & Logistics IT, even though it's in your main menu.
- Your About page says you're "based in the heart of Savannah, Georgia," while the rest of the page and your address say Rincon. Pick one and be consistent.
- You have ten well-built location pages — that's real groundwork. The same care hasn't yet reached your core service pages.

---

### What to Fix First

1. **Point the Managed IT menu item at your Savannah page** — puts your best-written, strongest-converting page in front of everyone who uses your menu.
2. **Replace "Get 3CX Now" with a call or quote button** — stops handing your highest-intent phone-system visitors to a vendor.
3. **Publish one consistent answer on after-hours support** — removes the contradiction standing between you and every prospect who asks the emergency question.
4. **Add hero call buttons to Managed IT, Cabling, and Alarm, and a CTA to the Services page** — applies the pattern your location pages already prove works.
5. **Build the cybersecurity page** — you already sell it, mention it constantly, and have nowhere to send anyone searching for it.
